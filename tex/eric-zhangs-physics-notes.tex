\section{The Electric Force}

\textbf{Electric charge} is denoted by the symbol \(q\) or sometimes \(Q\),
and can be either positive or negative.
Protons carry a positive charge and electrons carry a negative charge.

Its SI unit is the coulomb (C).
A coulomb is a rather large unit of charge.
One proton has a charge of \(\qty{1.602176634e-19}{\coulomb}\).
Conversely, one electron has a charge of \(\qty{-1.602176634e-19}{\coulomb}\).

Objects with a non-zero charge exert an \textbf{electric force} on particles around them;
the SI unit for electric field is a newton per coulomb (N/C).
Objects with charges of the same sign will repel each other,
while objects with charges of opposite signs will attract each other.
The case of neutral objects,
i.e., objects with an equal balance of charges is slightly more complicated
and will be explained later.
