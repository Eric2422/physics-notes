\section{Domains of Physics}

Before we can discuss "modern physics",
we have to discuss what is modern physics.
Physics can be divided into four domains.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{assets/domains-of-physics.pgf}}
    \caption{Domains of physics}
\end{figure}

Classical mechanics is the physics
that we are familiar with in our everyday lives,
e.g., Newton's laws of gravity, Maxwell's laws.
It accurately predicts phenomena that are relatively large and slow.

However, when phenomena become very small
(i.e., the size of atoms or smaller)
or very fast (i.e, approaching the speed of light~\(c\)),
physics becomes very different from what we are used to.
In these cases, classical mechanics breaks down,
and we need more accurate theories to replace them.
The theory of relativity applies large, fast-moving objects;
quantum mechanics to small, slow particles;
and quantum field theory to small, fast particles.
These three fields are collectively termed "modern physics".
