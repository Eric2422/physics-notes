\chapter{Moving Charges in Magnetic Fields}

Empirically, the following were observed about magnetic fields:
\begin{subequations}
    \begin{align}
        |\vec{F}| \propto q, \\
        \vec{F} \perp \vec{v}, \vec{B}, \\
        |\vec{F}| \propto \ab|\vec{v}|. \\
        |\vec{F}| \propto \sin\theta. \\
    \end{align}
\end{subequations}
These relationships can be expressed with the single equation
\begin{equation}
    \vec{F}_B = q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}.
\end{equation}
If we combine this with the electric force, the total electromagnetic force is
\begin{equation}
    \vec{F} = q (\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B}),
\end{equation}
which is the \textbf{Lorentz force law}.

If a charged particle moves through a uniform magnetic field,
the magnetic force serves as a centripetal force,
and the particle will be pushed into a circular orbit
(assuming the field remains constant and uniform).
We can find the find the radius to be
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        F &= \frac{m v^2}{r} \\
        q \vec{v} \times \vec{B} &= \frac{m v^2}{r} \\
        r &= \frac{m v^2}{q \vec{v} \times \vec{B}}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
