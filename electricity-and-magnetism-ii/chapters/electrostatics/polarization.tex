\chapter{Electric Polarization}

We have discussed how an object with a net charge
exerts an electric force upon objects in its surroundings.
However, it's also possible for an object with no net charge
to exert an electric force.
This may sound strange but it's actually fairly simple:
if the charges are asymmetrically distributed
in a way such that either positive or negative charges are closer,
the slight difference in distance will cause an electric force.

As an example, imagine the simplest case:
an \textbf{electric dipole},
which has a positive and negative charge separated by a distance.
